What We Study

Research

Physical mechanisms of climate variability, from theory to observation

Our research spans a range of spatial and temporal scales, unified by a common goal: understanding the physical mechanisms that govern climate variability and change. We use a hierarchy of approaches — idealized theory, numerical modeling, and observational analysis — to address questions relevant to both fundamental science and societal impact.

Atmospheric Circulation

We study the dynamics of jet streams, storm tracks, and planetary-scale waves, and how these features respond to radiative forcing. Current projects examine how a warming Arctic reshapes midlatitude circulation and the frequency of atmospheric blocking events.

  • Jet stream variability & midlatitude dynamics
  • Atmospheric blocking & persistent weather regimes
  • Arctic–midlatitude teleconnections

Ocean–Climate Interaction

The ocean stores and redistributes vast amounts of heat, shaping climate on seasonal to multi-decadal timescales. We investigate how variability modes such as ENSO and the Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation influence regional and global climate.

  • ENSO dynamics & global teleconnections
  • Ocean heat transport & meridional overturning circulation
  • Air–sea coupling & feedback mechanisms

Climate Modeling & Prediction

We develop and evaluate numerical models spanning idealized dynamical cores to comprehensive Earth system models, with a focus on improving seasonal-to-decadal predictability and reducing model biases in circulation and precipitation.

  • Model evaluation & bias diagnosis
  • Seasonal-to-decadal predictability
  • Idealized dynamical core experiments

Extreme Events

Heatwaves, droughts, and extreme precipitation events are becoming more frequent and intense under climate change. We study the dynamical and thermodynamical drivers of these extremes to improve understanding of their changing statistics.

  • Heatwave dynamics & attribution
  • Drought persistence & land–atmosphere feedbacks
  • Extreme precipitation under warming

Machine Learning for Climate

We apply data-driven and machine learning methods to accelerate climate model emulation, detect patterns in large observational and model datasets, and improve statistical downscaling of climate projections.

  • Climate model emulation
  • Pattern detection in high-dimensional climate data
  • Statistical & ML-based downscaling
